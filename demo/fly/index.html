koku · fly wardrobe

A fly with a perfume habit

Before every trip the fly gets a freshly shuffled wardrobe of twelve perfumes. The koku retriever scores each bottle against Turkish queries for where it is going (office, date, gym, night club, university, shopping mall), the fly sprays one of the bottles that fit, then tracks the odor plume there while its brain lights up.

    Wardrobe

      How picks stay sensible: a perfume counts as a fit for a place only if the fine-tuned BGE-M3 ranks it in the top 10% of its group (men's, women's or unisex) for that place's queries and its accords pass a simple rule (nothing heavy at the gym, nothing sharp and cold on a date, nothing fresh and quiet at the club, nothing heavy in class or in a crowded mall). Each wardrobe holds men's or women's perfumes (per the toggle) plus unisex: three fits and nine others; the fly samples among the fits, weighted by score. All scores were computed offline. The flight is a hand-written plume-tracking loop (surge upwind while in the odor, cast crosswind when it is lost, land by sight up close), the behavior real flies show. The brain glow is an illustrative mapping of that behavior onto the regions known to handle it (antennal lobes for smell, lateral horn and mushroom body for innate and learned odor value, central complex for steering, optic lobes for landing), not a simulation of the fruit fly connectome. 3D fly: NeuroMechFly (flygym), NeLy lab, EPFL, from a micro-CT scan of an adult Drosophila; Apache-2.0, simplified and re-rigged. Brain: FlyWire connectome of the adult female Drosophila brain (FAFB; FlyWire Consortium, Princeton), CC BY-NC 4.0; Dorkenwald et al. 2024 and Schlegel et al. 2024, Nature 634; neuropil meshes after Ito et al. 2014 (JFRC2); neuron skeletons from the public FAFB CATMAID. Perfume data: Fragrantica dataset on Kaggle (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0). Code: github.com/demirciberk/koku.
